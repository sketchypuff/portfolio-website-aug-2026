# Identity profile (working notes)

Raw notes from a self-discovery interview, used to choose the site's "world".
Not committed until Yash asks.

## Round 1 — Roots

**Place**
- Born in Ichalkaranji, Maharashtra; moved to Mumbai soon after. 26 years old, almost all of it in Mumbai.
- Grew up in Seawoods, Navi Mumbai — a 2BHK apartment in a building. Family still lives there.
- Family entirely Maharashtrian. Dad's side: Ratnagiri (Konkan coast). Mum's side: Pune.

**Language**
- Mother tongue Marathi, spoken at home.
- Dreams in English and Hindi. Swears in Hindi.
- Reads and writes all three scripts (Marathi and Hindi both Devanagari; plus Latin).

**The house**
- Small, bustling at 6am: up at 6, school bus at 7, school at 8.
- Morning smell: Mum cooking early — tiffins for Dad and sister.
- Would rescue from a fire: the **L-shaped sofa** — where the whole family gathered for lunch, dinner, morning and evening tea.

**Holidays**
- Every summer split between Nani's (Pune) and Dadi's (Ratnagiri).
- Train to both; Dad sometimes drove to Pune.
- Ratnagiri: frugal village home of brick and clay; **mangoes** (Ratnagiri = Alphonso country).
- Pune: cousins — "cousin time" — something he misses now.

## Round 1 — Obsessions, hands, rituals, the moment

**Obsessions (childhood)**
- Art, and tinkering: making models out of boxes and cardboard.
- A huge **anime** phase — now the exact opposite, doesn't watch any.
- **Cricket** — watched with Dad since childhood, same passion today. The most constant interest.
- Trained ~10 years combined in **hockey and badminton**.
- **Watercolour** then and now.

**Hands in the family**
- **Nana (maternal grandfather) was an artist — oil painter.** Mum dabbled in art.
- Nobody else makes things; Yash explored out of childhood boredom.

**Rituals**
- **Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali**, kept "with extreme rigor" — but what sticks is the atmosphere, not religious ritual: light, diyas, a good mood at home.

**The moment**
- 12th grade, preparing for IIT-JEE. Fell and cracked his tailbone; couldn't attend coaching.

- That's when the family discovered design as a career. Chose design over engineering → industrial design degree (NID) → pivoted to digital product design.

Favourites: **Naruto, One Piece**. Death Note had a good story but not those values. It switched off because study and then work took the time, not because he lost interest.

## Round 2 — Hands

**Watercolour today**
- **Landscapes only.** No people, objects or abstract — sticks to landscape by choice.
- **Wet and loose**, not careful or controlled.
- No paintings to share yet.

**Poetry**
- Wrote in **English**.
- About **things he wanted to say to someone but couldn't**: unrequited love, issues with partners, what he wanted to say to a crush before asking them out.

**How he designs**
- Paper and Figma side by side. When things are **messy**, he opens a notebook to sketch and write until there's **clarity**; once it's clear, it moves to Figma, where the fidelity work happens (colour, type, the final thing).
- No loyalty to a notebook, but loves his **Pilot Metropolitan fountain pen**: cheap for a fountain pen, writes extremely smoothly, unlike any he'd used before.

**Objects**
- The fountain pen.
- **Yonex Arcsaber 11 Pro**, his main badminton racket.
- A **Yamaha electronic keyboard**, still in its box; he needs to take it out.

**Materials (NID)**
- **Wood**: carves easily into many forms; lots of woodworking machines.
- **Metal**: drawn to **flat-pack** design, where the whole outline is cut from one sheet and bent into a 3D object.
- Both gave a **brutalist** feel. Attracted to brutalism: **the natural state of the material is the attraction, not enhancements applied to it.**

**Flow**
- Loses track of time in anything that needs **100% attention**: painting, badminton, reading. Half attention means doing it badly.
- Has skipped sleep reading, skipped meals painting, played badminton for hours more than he'd ever exercise otherwise.
- **Hates the end**: when thinking and ideating are done and he has to make the final thing, hand it off, and close the process.

## Round 2 — Follow-ups

- **Landscapes are neither imagined nor real.** He doesn't paint on location; he recreates good landscape photos, or other painters' paintings he comes across (he doesn't track who).
- **Brutalism** is in both architecture and objects for him, but mostly objects; he hasn't visited many brutalist buildings. He has visited **NID's campus and loves it**: brutalism applied in a **very Indian context**, not "communist and Soviet Union-like".
- **Keyboard**: wanted to learn composed music he can read off sheet music: classical pieces, anime soundtracks, classical music from films he heard once and saved to a playlist.

**Food and drink**

**Dislikes (hard constraints for the site)**
- No crazy motion.
- No huge type.
- No operating-system-style websites.
- No ultra-maximalism.
- Design elements placed **intentionally and sparingly**, so **the content (case studies, projects) is the hero, not the website's design**.
- Shouldn't look extravagant; should look **crafted and made with good intent**.

**Correction from Yash:** the site should **not be minimalist**. It should be **maximalism done tastefully**. "No ultra-maximalism" meant no chaos, not no richness. Read the dislikes above as limits on *how* it's maximal, not a vote for restraint.

**Benchmark for "the right amount": billguo.me.** What it does, from looking at it (2026-10-21):
- **Calm frame, rich object.** The top half is a quiet text grid: small mono labels (MENU, PROFILE, NOW), a serif menu, short body copy, generous space. All the richness sits in one place: a pile of postage stamps in the lower half.
- **One metaphor, used for real.** Projects *are* stamps, sorted into loose piles by discipline (product, visual, motion). It's how the work is organised, not decoration.
- **Material honesty.** Cream paper grain, perforated edges, halftone and print textures, real soft shadows. It looks like objects on a desk.
- **Culture lives inside the work.** The Chinese characters are on the stamps, plus a tiny red seal stamp in the header. No borders, patterns or motifs spread around the page.
- **Interaction rewards attention.** Hovering a stamp lifts it into focus, blurs the others, shows a small dark label, and lets a faint ink-brush botanical appear behind. Slow and quiet, not flashy.
- **Type stays modest.** A serif headline at normal size, mono labels, a sans for body copy. Nothing huge.
- **Fits on one screen.** The home page doesn't scroll; the stamp pile *is* the index.

**Framework (from Yash, 2026-10-21):** the question is still **"what's my world?"**, with a **prime motif** set inside it. Bill's world is his Asian roots; his motif is the stamp, which works twice: it's cultural, *and* stamps/seals were how branding worked historically, and he designs brand identities. A good motif = **an object from the world that is also a metaphor for the profession.**

**Framework, part 2 (from Yash, 2026-10-21):** the world must also be **relatable to the audience (designers)**: a recognisable aesthetic or movement, like Barbie + Y2K (Darshita) or China + stamps (Bill). Hyper-local references (Mumbai, Indian Railways, his childhood) won't land. Formula: **a recognisable aesthetic the audience already knows × Yash's authentic connection to it × a motif that doubles as a design metaphor.**

**Rejected worlds (2026-10-21):** Eames / mid-century modern (doesn't care for it), anime backgrounds (too busy or childish, no longer into anime), maker's workshop (doesn't practise industrial design anymore), sports trading cards (never collected them). Lesson: biographical facts aren't the same as current affinity. Build from what he relates to *now*, not what he once did.

**Leading direction:** sketchbook / field notes. The only option he relates to.

**Sketchbook flavours he likes (2026-10-21):**
- (a) the designer's working notebook (wireframes, arrows, messy middle)
- (b) ink and wash, pushed further into a watercolour world
- (c) field notes (observation → insight; UX research as fieldwork)
- (d) the reader's notebook, from the world of books

**Reading:** mostly **fiction**, no fixed genre: sci-fi, thriller, fantasy, literary fiction.

**Still to answer**: three places where he feels most himself; the core of the book hoard; people and sites he admires; whether he still writes poetry and would share it.

## Early patterns

- **Making is social.** Modak shaped together, the sofa, tea twice a day, cousin time, cricket with Dad. Home = people gathered around something being made or shared.
- **Painting is lineage, not hobby.** Nana's oils → Mum dabbling → Yash's watercolour, unbroken from childhood to now.
- **A happy accident made him a designer.** A cracked tailbone turned an engineering path into a painting one. Watercolour is itself the medium of happy accidents.
- **Hands first.** Cardboard models → industrial design → digital. The physical came before the screen.
- **Constants vs phases.** Cricket, watercolour, family festivals persist. Anime was crowded out by study and work, but its values (friendship, loyalty, not giving up) stayed.
- **Play inside tradition.** Modak bent into ducks, rats and monkeys: he takes a fixed ritual form and makes it playful. Same instinct as "opinionated and delightful".
- **Learning by someone dropping in.** Nana teaching in passing over his shoulder, not formally.
- **Mumbai pride.** Sachin, Rohit — both Mumbai players; shared with Dad.
- **Truth to materials.** Brutalism (material in its natural state), wet-and-loose watercolour (let water and paper behave), a smooth fountain pen (ink as ink). He likes things that show what they are made of. Probably the strongest design principle so far.
- **Loose and rigid are the same value.** Loose watercolour and brutalist metal look like opposites, but both let the material lead instead of forcing it.
- **Places, not people.** He paints only landscapes, yet his memories are all about people. Places are where he goes inward; people are what he holds onto.
- **The unsaid.** His poems are things he couldn't say aloud. There's a private, tender voice under the playful public one.
- **In love with the middle.** Flow lives in the messy, attentive middle; finishing and handing off is what he hates. A process-first, never-quite-finished site would be honest.
- **Messy → clear.** Notebook and fountain pen for chaos, Figma for clarity. Two modes, two tools.
- **Tasteful maximalism.** Rich, layered and abundant, but every element placed on purpose. Limits: calm motion, normal-sized type, no OS metaphor, no chaos, and the work stays readable as the hero. Abundance should come from texture, layers and detail, not volume or effects.
- **Indian modernism, not Indian ornament.** He loves NID's brutalism because it is Indian without being decorative. That's the register: rooted, honest, restrained.
- **Learns by recreating.** Paints from references, wants to play from sheet music, used to shape modak variations, learned from Nana dropping in. Craft through interpretation and practice, not invention from nothing.
- **Two Indias in his taste.** Quiet and material (NID, muted clothes, fountain pen) plus loud and street (Desi hip-hop, Hindi swearing, vada pav). Restrained surface, warm and street-level voice.
- **Konkan vs city.** Frugal brick-and-clay village with mangoes vs a dense Navi Mumbai apartment.
- **Trains** as the thread between the three places (Mumbai, Pune, Ratnagiri).
- **Light** — diyas, Diwali — is the remembered feeling, not ritual.
- **Marathi/Devanagari** is native, not decorative — bilingual type would be authentic, not borrowed.
